How it changed its mind

    
  

  

  
After each movement the composer listens back to what it actually wrote,
     scores it against named aesthetic principles, and shifts the parameters it writes with.
     Plasticity is how hard it reacts — it climbs when complaints persist and settles when
     they stop.

Rhythms it decided on

  

  
Each voice starts from the metrical hierarchy its meter implies, then
     auditions patterns against it — pulse, syncopation, how much it collides with what is
     already playing, whether it reads as a figure. After every movement the grid moves
     toward whatever it actually played, so a slot it keeps choosing ends up weighing more
     than the meter ever gave it.


  
What it owes

  

  
Every gesture opens an account — a leap owes a step back, a line stopping under
     a chord tone owes the note above it, a pattern that never lands the downbeat owes it, a
     progression that walks far from home owes a return. A promise ripens: worthless the
     instant it opens, worth most once the ear has been waiting, fading once nobody is waiting any
     more. That single curve is what makes "too soon" a number — settling early scores worse than
     carrying, which scores worse than settling something ripe.


  
What it listens for when it picks a line

  

  
Starting weights come from the mood. The critic teaches them: a principle
     that keeps failing makes its criterion matter more. Nothing pulls them back to where
     they began — moved marks the ones that left.
